### 4.1 Tech Words (Wordle-like, tech vocabulary)

Rules: guess a hidden tech term of 3, 4 or 5 letters (chosen before the round), 6 tries. Green = right letter in the right place, yellow = in the word but elsewhere, gray = not in the word; letters that are known to be absent are grayed out on the on-screen keyboard. **Only terms from our list are accepted as guesses** (an unknown word shakes the row and costs no try), so no third-party dictionary is needed. After the round a card explains the term in one sentence and shows its category. Optional category filter (languages, web, data, networking, hardware, OS, security, DevOps, ML, general computer science). Result can be copied as an emoji grid.

- [x] **Data (a lot of care):** our own lists in `src/games/tech-words/data/`, each entry with the term (lowercase a to z), a category and a definition of at most 100 characters. ### 4.2 Root Cause (logic puzzle in the style of a murder mystery book)

Our own puzzles for a placement-and-deduction game. **The rules, the full clue catalog, the design decisions and the measurements live in `src/games/root-cause/README.md`; that file is the source of truth, this plan only keeps the summary.**

- **Board and people:** a square N by N map (4 by 4 up to 9 by 9), N people (N minus 1 suspects and one victim), exactly one person in every row and every column. Shapes other than squares come later.
- **Rooms:** connected, at least 2 cells, compact; at most one corridor (a strip) per map. The number of rooms has a lower limit and a generous upper limit per size; the tier only leans (easy more rooms, hard fewer). Every room has a **type and a name** that fit the skin (several of a type allowed, numbered then); clues can speak about a type. The culprit is the suspect who is alone with the victim in a room.
- **Objects and windows:** occupiable objects (landmarks to stand on) and blocking objects, chosen to fit the room. Easy maps have more places to stand and more blocked cells (less free space, so it is clearer where people can be), hard maps have more open floor. Windows sit on walls (board edge or room boundary).
- **People:** Case file gives every person a random first name (never two the same, unique initials for the notes) and a job as role; Outage numbers devices (`sw-01`, `sw-02`, `fw-01`), the victim is a server, a database, a NAS or another device that no suspect shares a type with (`srv-01`, `db-01`), in Case file a founder, CEO, developer and so on (the plan sets the victim per level). Names and labels are unique within every level (tested, also for generated levels).
- **Clues:** rare, as few as needed, every suspect has at least one, **the victim never gets a clue and is never named**, kinds are mixed (no kind dominates, also on hard levels). About 45 types in three levels: about one suspect, combinations (and, or) and **neutral clues about the whole map** (0 to 3 per level, only where needed). Easy levels use level 1, medium 1 and 2, hard all three. "Next to" means the four neighbors inside the same room, a wall is the board edge or a room boundary. **Play-testing decides which types stay**, each type can be dropped in one list.
- **Marking:** the player places a person in a cell, crosses cells out, and writes **notes**: small, faint initials of the people who could be there, several per cell; a switch changes between place, note and cross mode. There is no instant feedback; the game recognizes a solution only when every person is placed correctly, then the culprit is revealed.
- **Skins** (one engine, two looks, both are built, decided by Leon on 2026-10-08): **Case file** (our own detectives, suspects and rooms in tech settings: server room, open office, break room) and **Outage** (network version: rooms are network zones, the victim is the failed device, for example always a server, the suspects are devices such as router, switch, firewall and NAS, and the root cause is the device that is alone with the failed one in a zone). Each skin has its own object set with its own names, icons and clue wording (Outage: racks, fans, radiators, UPS, cable trays and so on; which are occupiable is decided with the skin design). The logic never knows the skin: a skin maps abstract object kinds (occupiable, blocked, window) to its names and icons. The separate network game (4.5) comes later and is a different game.
- [x] 4.2a **Engine** in `src/games/root-cause/logic/` (done 2026-10-09, game still hidden; details in `src/games/root-cause/README.md`): board and maps (rooms with fitting names and types, several of a type allowed, a generous room count with only a lean by tier, compact rooms and at most one corridor, windows on walls, objects that fit the room), about 45 clue types in three levels (about a suspect, combinations with and/or, and neutral clues about the whole map, 0 to 3 per level depending on the tier) with checks that answer true, false or not decided yet, a **search solver**, a second **rule solver** that works the way a person does and doubles as the **rater** (reasoning level 1 to 3, level 4 is rejected as too hard), and a **generator** that adds clues until the rater can solve the puzzle at the wanted level and then removes every clue that is not needed (every suspect keeps one, kinds mixed: at least three kinds, none above a third, hard clues at most 40 percent). `problemsOf` lets both solvers verify every level. Tests: several hundred for this engine, including both solvers agreeing on 100+ clue combinations and verified puzzles of every tier in both skins. Measured: easy up to 7 by 7 and medium up to 9 by 9 in seconds, hard up to 9 by 9 in seconds to minutes (fine for an offline level pool); easy puzzles from 8 by 8 are rare. Shapes other than squares are not built yet.
- [x] 4.2b **Level pool** (done 2026-10-10) in `src/games/root-cause/data/levels.json`: **18 levels, six per tier** (easy 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6; medium 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7; hard 7, 7, 8, 8, 9, 9; skins alternate; the plan is `data/pool-plan.ts`), made by the engine with fixed seeds (`pnpm levels`, a script that runs through vitest, keeps existing levels and takes a few minutes), each with a title and a short story in `data/stories.ts`. The pool leaves out maps with too few rooms (`minRoomsOf`). Level gate in CI (`pool.test.ts`): the pool equals the plan, every level passes `problemsOf` (both solvers find exactly the stored solution), the rater puts it into its tier, no clue could be taken away, every level has a story, no two maps are equal, the file stays small. Shapes and random maps come later.
- [x] 4.2c **UI** (done 2026-10-10, game still hidden; details in `src/games/root-cause/README.md`, "The interface"): React island with the map as an accessible grid (room colors from the site's tokens, walls, windows, own drawings of the objects), people list, place, note and cross modes with keyboard shortcuts (a person crosses out their row and column by themselves and everything comes back when they leave; choosing a person leaves the cross mode), undo and redo, clear, clues that tick themselves while the people on the map satisfy them, a **page for the choice of a level** (the 18 levels as cards and the **random level**; the level is a page of its own with a way back), a legend of all pictures, room colors by kind of room (one color per room type), a result box of fixed height, and the **random level** (difficulty and size, generated in up to four Web Workers with a progress bar and estimate, every level checked by both solvers, titles and stories from templates), the result with the stamp and the culprit pointed out. ### 4.3 Word Radar (similarity game, working name for the Semantle and Contexto style game)

Guess words, the game shows how close each is to a hidden word as a **rank** (1 is the hidden word) out of a vocabulary of about 30,000 common English words, with a color and a position on a radar-like display (closer means nearer to the centre). Unlimited rounds with a random hidden word, a hint button (reveals a word at a chosen rank), and "give up" (shows the nearest words). After the round a **map of the guesses** (a two-dimensional projection of the embedding space) shows where the guesses were relative to the hidden word, and a short "How it works" block explains GloVe vectors, PCA and cosine similarity. This is the game that talks about Leon's machine learning background.

- [ ] 4.3a **Data pipeline** (Python with NumPy, in `scripts/games/word-radar/`, the output is committed): GloVe 6B, 100 dimensions (public domain license) for the vocabulary and vectors, the first 30,000 words in frequency order, cleaned (letters only, no names, no stop words), reduced to 64 dimensions with PCA and stored as 8 bit integers (about 2 MB), plus a list of about 3,000 secret words (common nouns from WordNet, no offensive words). Manual step for Leon: download the GloVe zip once (about 800 MB) into a gitignored folder and run the script
- [ ] 4.3b Logic in TypeScript: cosine similarity and ranking of all words in the browser at the start of a round (a few milliseconds), validation of guesses, hints, nearest words; tested with a small fixture and with data checks (sizes, sorted, secrets are in the vocabulary, a few sanity pairs such as king and queen)
- [ ] 4.3c UI: radar display with rings and pulses, ranked guess list with color bar, hint, give up, post-game map (SVG), "How it works" block. Data is fetched when the page opens and the page shows a loading state; size budget about 3 MB
- Done when: a round can be played end to end, data tests pass, `THIRD_PARTY.md` credits GloVe and WordNet.

### 4.4 Link Up (grouping game, Connections style)

Sixteen terms, find the four groups of four. Unlimited random puzzles from a **pool of at least 40 categories with 10 or more members each** (tech first: sorting algorithms, HTTP methods, databases, things with a stack, protocols, ML models, Linux commands, and so on, reusing the Tech Words data where it fits). The generator picks four categories and four members each and rejects a puzzle if a word could belong to two of the chosen groups, so every puzzle has exactly one solution (the same idea as the solver of Root Cause). Four mistakes allowed, groups are revealed with color and a short sweep animation, the player can shuffle.

- [ ] Data and generator with tests (no ambiguity, pool size, every category documented), UI with swap, shake and reveal animations, keyboard play, e2e, axe, README
- Done when: random puzzles are always valid and solvable and the game is playable with keyboard and touch.
