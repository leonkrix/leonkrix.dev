# Backbone

A planning game about networks: you route traffic through a small network, then a stress test judges the plan. Our own game, our own levels, texts and drawings. The concept (the reasoning behind every rule) is in [docs/network-game-concept.md](../../../docs/network-game-concept.md).

Status: **built and live in this branch** (set to `live` in the registry; it is merged after Leon's okay). The model, the stress test, the exact solver, the generator, the campaign pool (13 chapters, 78 levels), the interface, the overview page, random levels in five tiers, the note, the browser tests and the Lighthouse budget are done. Open: polish (fonts, animation, real packets instead of dots), see PLAN.md, 4.5.

## Rules

- A level is a **network** (nodes and cables with a capacity, a delay and sometimes a loss), a list of **flows** and one to three **situations**.
- A flow goes from a node to a node and has a **service profile** (`logic/profiles.ts`): voice must be fast, control must arrive, a transaction cannot lose anything, video wants steady bandwidth, web wants a bit of everything, bulk does not care. A profile is a weight, a latency budget with a steepness, a loss exponent, a queue class and whether it can be held back.
- You draw a path for every flow. Options appear with the chapters: **split** a flow over up to three paths in quarters, a **backup** path that is taken at once when the path in use is cut, **redundancy** (0, 25 or 50 percent extra coded packets that recover loss), **throttling** of bulk traffic (a slider in steps of 10 %; the solver searches exactly the same ten steps, `THROTTLE_STEPS` in `logic/options.ts`, so nothing the slider can do beats the best plan), and a **change of path at a minute**. A second path and a backup are **drawn by the player**: "Add path" and "Add a backup path" start as a copy of the first path, selected, and the player clicks the nodes of the route. Paths, the backup and the throttle are a list: one row per path with its share, "+25 %" (from the biggest path that has more than a quarter) and a bin.
- The **stress test** (`logic/simulate.ts`) is deterministic. In every step the flows send along their paths; on every cable the queues are served in order (voice and control, then the middle class, then bulk) and what does not fit is dropped; a flow is as fast as the narrowest cable on its path. A flow earns `weight x demand x delivered^k x timeliness` per step. A cut cable loses the traffic of the flows on it for two steps unless they have a backup, then the network reroutes them. **Delay** of a cable is `delay x (1 + load^2)` plus waiting in buffers, so a full cable is slow for everybody, also for traffic that is served first; that is why holding bulk traffic back helps (it is served last and dropped first, but while it fills a cable it makes that cable slow). The player sees milliseconds (one unit is 5 ms, `logic/latency.ts`): the cables show their delay under the load from the chapter about latency on, the panel adds up the delay of the route being drawn against what the kind of traffic can take, and the result says when a flow was too slow (`LateFlow` in `logic/text.ts`).
- **Radio links** (event `range`, chapter 10, `logic/simulate.ts` `outOfRange`): a cable that is in range only in a window of a situation. Outside it the cable is not there (`LinkStepResult.absent`, it counts as down, drawn dashed in amber like a cable that is not built, label "away"). The generator (`moving`) makes windows that meet in the middle of the situation, so one change of route takes a flow from the links that were there to those that come, and the network connects every flow in both halves.
- The **score** of a plan is the mean of its situations with the worst one counted twice, from 0 to 100.
- **Stars** (`logic/stars.ts`): one star for at least the shortest path, two for beating both simple strategies and being 40 percent of the way to the best plan, three for coming within 5 percent of the distance to the best plan. A level is only admitted when those steps are real (`isAdmissible`).
- **Helpers** ("Shortest for all", "Balance for me") fill in the plan; a run with a helper earns no stars. A solved level can load the best plan.
- **Chapters** (13, six levels each): capacity, sharing, splitting, latency, priorities, **queues**, failures and backup paths, **building** (optional cables with a cost and a budget, `logic/build.ts`), loss and redundancy, moving links (a change of path at a step), **zones** (a flow can have to pass a node, the firewall, `Flow.via`), **an attack** (a flood, `logic/flood.ts`), everything. `Level.rules` says which options a level offers.
- **Queues** (`logic/queues.ts`, from chapter 6 on; one queue per cable since 2026-10-19): every cable has one queue as big as its nominal capacity (what it carries in a step, `bufferSize`), the same rule for every cable, nothing is random. What does not go through a link in a step waits in the queue and goes on in a later step. The queue is served by priority (voice and control, then web, video and payments, then bulk); when more waits than it holds, the classes that come last are dropped first (`room` in `simulate.ts` and `fast.ts`, the classes served first keep their place). A unit of backlog adds `QUEUE_DELAY` units of delay for every step the link needs to work it off, and traffic waits behind the classes that are served before it. Without the rule the queue is empty, which is the model of the first chapters. Under every cable the map shows a small bar and "Q 3/5" (`LinkStepResult.queued`, `buffer`; `queuedBy` splits it by class for the screen reader). The solver relies on the same property as before: extra traffic only makes the backlog bigger, and never helps a flow that is already in the plan.
- **A flood** (`logic/flood.ts`): useless traffic from one node to another, along the fastest whole path, in the middle queue class; the first firewall on its way stops it. The player cannot route it, only keep their own traffic out of its way.
- **Hints** (`logic/hint.ts`, at most 120 characters each so that they fit the status line, a pool test checks it): three steps, the cable that is overfull, who uses it, and what to try (the first difference from the best plan; this step gives the answer away, so a run after it earns no stars).
- **Random levels** (`logic/random-level.ts`): five tiers, made in background threads (`components/generate.worker.ts`), only shown after the best plan was proven and the level passed the same checks as the campaign. The address (`?level=random&tier=3&seed=...`) makes the same level again.
- Unlimited tries, nothing is saved.

## Design decisions

- **The solver proves the best plan** (`logic/solver.ts`): branch and bound over the flows. It relies on one property of the model, tested in `simulate.test.ts`: extra traffic never helps a flow that is already in the plan, so what the placed flows earn in a partial plan is an upper bound. `logic/fast.ts` is the same stress test on arrays for the solver; a test compares it with the readable one on 120 random plans. The solver is tested against trying every combination (`solver.test.ts`).
- **The rules of a level decide the options** (`Level.rules`): the interface shows only those, and the solver only looks at plans that use only those, so a level of chapter 3 is proven with the options of chapter 3.
- **The two simple strategies** (`logic/baselines.ts`) know nothing about failures, loss or priorities: one path per flow, no extras.
- **Levels come from a generator and are admitted by the solver.** `logic/generate.ts` makes a well formed level from a spec and a seed (a planar network of one of four families, flows of the given profiles, situations). `data/campaign-plan.ts` is the plan of the campaign (chapters, level titles and stories, the spec, and which options the best plan has to use so that the new rule of a chapter matters). `pnpm levels-backbone` searches a seed for every level, proves the best plan and writes `data/levels.json`.
- **Drawing:** the map is an SVG for the cables, the paths and the packets, with real buttons on top for the nodes (focus, keyboard, touch target of 44 pixels). The load of a cable is a color, a dashed line when it is over capacity, and always a number. The packets are moved by the component frame by frame; with reduced motion the stress test is still frames.
- **Which flow is which:** every flow has a number, in the list, in the panel of the flow and on the map. A filled number sits on the node where the flow starts, an outlined one where it ends (top right corner of the node, `MapView.tsx`); the colour is the kind of traffic. The label placement (`logic/labels.ts`) keeps the usual side of a name and, where the name would lie on the numbers, lifts it by one badge (`Placement.lifted`, only names above a node can be hit); the "from" and "to" marks treat the numbers as obstacles and the border of the map is kept free for both (`pool.test.ts` fails on a level where a name or a mark has no free place, and the generator rejects such a seed). The nodes of a level are stretched over the whole map inside a border (`logic/lattice.ts`), so a level never uses only a part of it. When a flow gets its first route the next flow without one is selected (`play.ts`, `nextUnrouted`). In the generator the flows of a level send different amounts (`spreadDemands`: two flows differ, three or more show at least three values, video and bulk stay the big ones, voice and control the small ones; `pool.test.ts` checks it).
- **Labels hold on every width, and a test measures it.** Everything drawn on the map (nodes, names, marks, numbers of flows, loads) is sized in `em` of one font size that is a 54th of the width of the map (`.bb-map > *`, 10.9 px from 590 px up, 8.8 px at the least), so everything is as big compared with the map on every width, and the places that `logic/labels.ts` finds hold on all of them. The placement also chooses the corner of the numbers (left when the right one would reach the border), where along its cable a load is shown (`Placement.loads`, a little aside when the middle is taken) and lifts names that touch numbers. `tests/e2e/backbone-labels.spec.ts` measures the page for every level and every selectable flow, at 1280 and at 880 pixels (the narrowest page with two columns): nothing lies outside the map, nothing lies on another label, on a node (except the numbers on their own node) or on a cable (except a load on its own cable and numbers on the cables of their node). On a phone (a map under 480 px) only "inside the map" is checked: a node alone is 40 of 340 pixels there. A level whose labels cannot be placed is not kept by the pool script (`placeNames` and `tagConflicts` must be 0, `pool.test.ts` checks it again), so after a change of the sizes run `pnpm exec vitest run src/games/backbone/pool.test.ts` and make **only the levels that fail** again (`LEVELS=c3-4 LEVELS_FORCE=1 pnpm levels-backbone`, for several levels in parallel with `LEVELS_OUT` and `LEVELS_JOIN`, file names as `C:/...` on Windows).
- **Nothing is hidden before the run** (decided 2026-10-19, so that nobody has to run a level to learn what happens in it): the panel has a forecast, "What will happen", with every situation and its events minute by minute (`forecastOf` in `logic/text.ts`, `components/Forecast.tsx`), and the cables something happens to carry a dashed halo in the colour of the event on the map while the player plans (`riskyLinks`). Uncertainty is not hidden but made a list: a level can have several situations ("a cable is cut" in more than one place) and a plan is judged on all of them, the worst counting twice. The plan lists a kind twice for that (`calm cut cut`, `calm flood flood`): the generator makes two situations of one kind that cannot take the same cable or the same attacker (`taken` in `eventsFor`), called "Cable cut A" and "Cable cut B" (ids `cut` and `cut-2`). Levels with two possible failures: c7-3, c7-5, c8-6, c9-4, c11-3, c12-3, c12-5, c13-1 (not every level, so the single failure stays the usual case; the limit is still three situations per level). The stress test counts in **minutes** for the player (shown from 1; in the model it is the step with the index minute minus 1).
- **Result.** The stars are decided on the points that are shown (`pointsOf`, 76 against 76 is a star). The verdict says "as good as" when the score equals a simple strategy or the best plan (no "better than"). When the score is within a point of the best plan, "Where your plan fell short" becomes "What it costs", in the colour of information: nothing better is possible, so it is not a fault; a flow the player holds back (throttle) is said to be held back, not lost. With more than one situation the panel lists the score of every situation (the worst one "counts twice") and the total, which is what the stars come from. The buttons "Change the plan", "Load the best plan" and "Next level" are in one row under the panel, on the height of the play bar. The best plan stays hidden until a star is earned, the card says so.
- **Building by routing** (chapter 8 on): clicking or dragging over a cable that is not built yet builds it, if the budget pays for it (`buildOnTheWay` in `play.ts`), and the nodes it leads to are marked as next. The "Build" chips on the cables do not move on hover.
- **Packets** (decided 2026-10-19, `packetsAt` in `logic/view.ts`). A path sends one packet per unit of traffic and minute (at most twelve, redundancy included: the extra ones are small, empty boxes, "coded" packets), spread evenly over the minute. A packet crosses every cable in the time that cable takes, its delay (and longer while it is full) times `MINUTES_PER_DELAY`, so a long route has more packets under way at once and the gaps between them stay the same; two paths with equal shares send equally many. Packets of a burst go on to their target when the burst is over. A cable that loses 20 % makes 20 % of the packets that cross it fade, a little bigger, in the middle of that cable, spread evenly over the packets (`dropsAt`); packets that a full cable cannot take (`UsedPath.kept`) fade where it is full. The marks of a flood run the way the flood goes (`floodDirections`). Kinds of traffic appear in the chapter that needs them (web from 1, voice from 4, bulk from 5, video from 6, payments from 9, control from 12) and the text of what a flow wants names only what the chapters before have taught (`wantsOf`).
- **Another route during the stress test** (rule "stages"): a flow can switch to another route from a minute on, for example when a cable is cut (`Plan` stage with `from` the step). The minutes offered are the ones where an event of a situation starts or ends (`switchSteps`); the panel says which event that is ("Reroute from minute 4 (Core – Hub A is cut)", `describeSwitch` in `logic/text.ts`), and the new route is drawn on the map like the first one.
- **The way on** (the button "Next level") is under the panel, on the height of the play bar, and the panel is as high as the map and its status (a grid with explicit rows in the wide layout, `.bb-stage`); "Change the plan" and "Load the best plan" stay in the panel.
- **The result is a list of blocks, each with a heading:** "Your result" (score, stars, what they mean), "Where your plan fell short" (the cable the plan overloaded, in which situation, and the flow that lost the most; "How your plan held up" when nothing went wrong), "What each flow earned" (a table whose rows pick the flow shown on the map) and "Your plan next to the others" (the four small maps and what the two simple strategies do). The status line of the stress test shows the step and what is going on in it as small coloured tags (cut and flood red, loss and slowdown amber, burst blue).
- **Layout never moves:** a skeleton with the same boxes is rendered on the server (`BackboneSkeleton.tsx`), the panel on the right has a fixed height and scrolls inside.
- **Data:** the level pool (`data/levels.json`) is fetched when a level of the campaign is opened, after the page has been painted (`data/load.ts`), like the data of Word Radar.

## Data (sources and licenses)

Everything is our own. Icons are from Lucide (ISC, see THIRD_PARTY.md), copied into `data/icons.ts`. The names in the settings (`data/settings.ts`) are plain words.

## How to add levels

1. Add an entry to `LEVEL_PLAN` in `data/campaign-plan.ts` (id `c<chapter>-<number>`, setting, title, story, shape, `requires`).
2. Run `LEVELS=c3-5 pnpm levels-backbone`. Existing levels are kept unless `LEVELS_FORCE=1`. `LEVELS_OUT`, `LEVELS_CANDIDATES`, `LEVELS_PROOF_MS` and `LEVELS_JOIN` are described at the top of `scripts/generate-backbone-levels.gen.ts` (several runs can work in parallel and be merged).
3. Look at the level in `pnpm dev` and play it. The script also writes `data/catalog.json` (what the overview needs); `pool.test.ts` checks the pool and the catalog against the plan.

A change of the generator, the model or the settings changes the levels: run the script again (`LEVELS_FORCE=1`) and commit the pool.

Browser tests: `tests/e2e/backbone.spec.ts` (clicks, keyboard, helpers, the stress test, building, random levels, storage, axe, reduced motion).
