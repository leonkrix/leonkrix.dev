# Root Cause

A logic puzzle of placement and deduction: work out who was alone with the victim. Our own game, our own levels, characters and texts. The rules follow the kind of "murder mystery" puzzles from puzzle books; nothing is copied from any book.

Status: **live** at `/games/root-cause/`. The engine (`logic/`), the level pool (`data/levels.json`, 18 levels) and the interface (`components/`, with random levels and shareable links) are built.

## Rules

- The board is a square of **N by N** cells (4 by 4 up to 9 by 9). Exactly **N people** stand on it, **one in every row and one in every column**: N minus 1 suspects and the **victim**.
- The board is divided into **rooms**. The **culprit is the suspect who is alone with the victim in a room**: the room of the victim holds the victim and exactly one suspect.
- **Every room has a type and a name** that fit together ("Server room", "Break room", "Open office" in Case file, "Server room", "Network closet", "Data hall" in Outage). Several rooms can have the same type on one map, then they are numbered ("Server room 1", "Server room 2"), and clues can speak about the type ("was in no server room").
- **Rooms** are connected and have at least two cells. The number of rooms has a lower limit that grows with the board (2 for 4 by 4 and 5 by 5, 3 for 6 by 6 and 7 by 7, 4 for 8 by 8 and 9 by 9) and a generous upper limit (4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 9). Within the limits every count is possible on every level, the tier only leans: easy levels towards more rooms, hard levels towards fewer. The rooms are compact, but about one map in three has a **corridor**, a straight strip (never more than one per map, and no other room is a strip).
- Cells can hold **objects**. Some can be stood on (chair, rug, sofa that covers two cells in Case file; rack, patch panel, cable tray in Outage), the others block their cell (desk, screen, plant, shelf, crate, printer; radiator, fan, UPS, cabinet). **Rooms are named after their size**: every kind of room has the fewest and the most cells it can have (an open office or a data hall has at least five, a meeting room at most eight, a network closet at most six) and a "bigness" (a hall is big, a closet small); among the allowed kinds the ones that like the size of the room are likelier, and the biggest rooms of a map choose their kind first. **Every room has objects, and a bigger room has more** (placed room by room: at least one object for up to five cells, then one more for every five cells further, so a room of two cells always has exactly the one it needs; the target is about a third of the cells on easy maps, a quarter on medium and a fifth on hard ones, multiplied by a density per kind of room: a server room or an archive is fuller than a reception or a corridor). About **half of the objects can be stood on and half block a cell**, and a room with two or more objects has at least one of each. The first object of each sort in a room is the **typical** one for its kind of room (a server in a server room, a rack in a data hall): the fit to the room counts squared for it. Several objects of one kind can be in the same room (a few racks in a server room). **Objects fit the room**: a sofa is likely in a break room and never in a server room. The tier changes how much of the map is usable: **easy maps have more places to stand and more blocked cells** (less free space, so it is clearer where people can be), **hard maps have fewer of both and more open floor**. A **window** (a vent in Outage) sits on a wall, on the edge of the board or on a room boundary.
- **Clues** say something true about a suspect (a clue about a person), or about the map as a whole (a **neutral** clue). **The victim never gets a clue and is never named.** Neutral clues count everybody, the victim included. Clues are rare: as few as needed, every suspect has at least one, and the kinds of clues differ from person to person and from level to level.
- The player puts people on cells, crosses cells out and writes notes. The game recognizes a solution only when everybody is placed correctly, then the culprit is revealed.
- A wall is the edge of the board or a room boundary. **Next to** means the four directly adjacent cells (never diagonal) in the **same room**. **An object is one unit**, also a sofa or a cable tray that covers two cells: a person is next to it when a cell beside them belongs to it, but not when they stand on it (then its other half is beside them, which does not count); "on the sofa" is a clue of its own. A direction from an object is taken from the whole object ("north of the sofa" is north of every cell of it, so of its top cell),, and "in the same row or column as" counts every cell of the object, also the one the person stands on (the object lies in that row, whether it is occupied or not). Single cell objects behave as before, apart from this last point (a person on a chair is in the same row as a chair).

### The clues

Every clue has a level that says how hard it is to use (a few depend on their numbers). Easy levels use only level 1 clues, medium levels level 1 and 2, hard levels all three. A level can have 0 to 3 neutral clues: easy 0 to 1, medium 0 to 2, hard 1 to 3. A neutral clue only stays when it is needed.

About a suspect:

| Level | Clue                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | in room X, in a room of type X, in a room with a window, in a room that has an object of a kind, on an object of a kind, on any piece of furniture                                                |
| 1     | in front of a window, in a corner of the map, in the first, last, middle or **numbered row or column** ("in the third row"), in the same room as another person, alone in the room, with n others |
| 2     | not in room X, not in a room of type X, not on an object of a kind, on the bare floor, the only person on a kind of object, in the smallest or largest room                                       |
| 2     | next to or not next to an object of a kind, next to or not next to a wall, **in a corner of their room, in a corner of a given room**, not in the same room as another person                     |
| 2     | an object of a kind in the same row or column, in the same room or in another room                                                                                                                |
| 2     | south-west (or any of the eight directions) of an object of a kind, in the same room, in another room or anywhere                                                                                 |
| 2     | with at least, exactly or none of a role in the room (a role is a job in Case file and a device type in Outage)                                                                                   |
| 2     | exactly one step or anywhere north, south, east, west, north-east, north-west, south-east or south-west of another person, diagonally adjacent                                                    |
| 2     | **two clues in one (and)**, for example "next to a plant and on a sofa" or "in a corner and south of X"                                                                                           |
| 3     | **one of two clues (or)**, between two other people (rows or columns), exactly n cells away from another person                                                                                   |

About the whole map (neutral):

| Level | Clue                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | nobody is on an object of a kind, nobody of a role is next to an object of a kind                                                                                                                     |
| 2     | no room is empty, a room is empty or holds exactly n people, at least or exactly n people are on a kind of object, no two people of a role share a room, these suspects are in different rooms        |
| 3     | exactly n rooms are empty, exactly n objects of a kind are free, every room holds a different number of people, every room has exactly n people on a kind of object, exactly n people are in a corner |

More types can be added without touching the solvers: a type needs an entry in `logic/types.ts`, a check in `logic/clues.ts` (true, false or not decided yet for a placement that is not complete), a level, a test, and (later) a text per skin. The generator offers every type and the pruning removes what is not needed; **play-testing decides which types stay**.

## Design decisions

- **The engine knows nothing about looks.** An object is a kind that is occupiable or not, a room has a type, a person has a role, a label and a name. A skin (`data/skins.ts`) supplies the kinds, the room types (with how well each kind of object fits), the roles and the names: **Case file** (detectives and suspects in tech workplaces, random first names, the role is a job) and **Outage** (the failed server is alone with the device that caused it, devices are numbered like `sw-01`, `sw-02`, `fw-01`). **The victim varies**: Case file has a founder, a CEO, an investor, a developer and so on, Outage a server, a database, a NAS, a router, a firewall or a load balancer (`skin.victimRoles`). Nobody else in the level has the role of the victim, so the victim is always `srv-01`, `db-01` and so on. The pool plan fixes the victim of every level; a random level picks one.
- **Four pieces, two solvers.**
  - `logic/clues.ts`: what a clue means. Every check answers true, false or "not decided yet", so a search can drop a branch as soon as a clue says no.
  - `logic/solver.ts`: solves by **search** (the person with the fewest possible cells first) and counts solutions. The cells that the clues about one person allow are worked out once.
  - `logic/propagation.ts`: solves **the way a person does**: sets of possible cells and rules that strike cells. It is the **rater** (what reasoning does a puzzle need?) and a second solver that shares no search code with `solver.ts`.
  - `logic/generator.ts`: makes puzzles and checks levels (`problemsOf`). `logic/layout.ts` makes the maps.
- **Difficulty is measured, not guessed.** The rater reports the highest reasoning that was needed: 1 singles and clues about one person, 2 clues that relate people, empty rooms, counting people on a kind of object and the rule about the victim, 3 "what if" (try a cell and find a contradiction). A puzzle that needs more than that is rejected as too hard for a player. The tier of a puzzle is the harder of its reasoning and its clues.
- **Generated by construction.** The generator chooses a map, a solution, and then clues until the rater can solve the puzzle at the wanted level, then takes away every clue that is not needed, keeping one for every suspect. The same seed always gives the same puzzle.
- **Every part of a clue has to say something.** A clue about one person that is true of every cell the person could stand on is never made, and in a combination ("and", "or") each part has to rule out cells that the other part does not (with "or": allow cells that the other does not). A part that follows from the other ("north-east of a rack and not on a patch panel", when no patch panel is north-east of a rack) is left out. This is checked for the parts that depend on the cell of one person alone; `pool.test.ts` checks it for every level.
- **At most one clue about a row and one about a column in a level** (the first, last, middle and numbered rows and columns together), and they are picked rarely, so that no three people are pinned down by their rows. **A row clue and a column clue never belong to the same person** (together they name a single cell, which is one clue's worth of information for the price of two; `axisSubjects`, checked by `pool.test.ts` and a generator test).
- **An easy level needs nothing but "this person has one cell left".** The rater has a plainer measure than `directlySolved`: `nakedSolved` places a person who has a single cell after the clues about them alone, takes the row and the column of that cell from everybody else, and goes on with the next person who has a single cell. No person bound to a row, no row with one place left, no clue about two people, no rule about rooms. **Every easy level has to be solved completely that way** (the victim, who has no clue, gets the last cell), so an easy level never asks for crossing out in the head; the generator and `pool.test.ts` enforce it. Those techniques (bound to a row, hidden single) belong to medium.
- **A gentle way in on a medium level.** The rater also says how many people the clues alone place, without the rules about rooms (`directlySolved`: clues about one person and about people next to each other, one person per row and column). Easy levels are solved that way completely, and even simpler (see the next point); on a **medium level at least half of the people** have to be placed by the clues alone, and the rules about rooms (the rule about the victim, empty rooms, counting) only finish the rest. Hard levels have no such condition and may need what-if.
- **A mixed set of clues.** Clues are picked with a weight (distance and between are picked rarely, they are strong but tiring to read), kinds that were used already are less likely, and a level is rejected unless it has at least three kinds, no kind takes up more than a third of six or more clues, and the hardest clues are not more than 40 percent.
- **Verified before it counts.** `problemsOf(level)` checks the structure (people, labels, names, room names, rows and columns, blocked cells, every suspect has a clue, nobody mentions the victim) and then needs **both solvers** to find exactly one solution, equal to the stored one. Generated levels in the tests and, later, every level of the pool pass it.

## The level pool

`data/levels.json` holds 18 levels, six per tier, made by the generator with fixed seeds and committed. `data/pool-plan.ts` says which level has which skin, tier, size and victim, `data/stories.ts` gives each a title and a short story (a story names the victim by role or device, as the plan sets it, but never a suspect or a room: those are made by the generator), and `pool.ts` turns a stored record back into a level for the engine.

- **Make or change levels:** `pnpm levels` makes the levels that are missing from the file and keeps the others. `LEVELS=hard-5` limits it to some levels, `LEVELS_FORCE=1` makes them again, `LEVELS_OUT=file.json` writes to another file (to make several levels in parallel and put the files together). The script tries the seeds `<id>-1`, `<id>-2` and so on until a puzzle passes `problemsOf` and has enough rooms (`minRoomsOf` in `pool-plan.ts`: the engine allows two rooms, the pool does not), and stores the seed that worked.
- **Change the plan:** edit `pool-plan.ts`, add the story, run `pnpm levels`. A new level is nothing more than a new entry.
- **Why the script runs as a test file:** vitest resolves our TypeScript imports, plain Node cannot (imports without file extensions). It has its own config, `vitest.levels.config.ts`, and never runs with `pnpm test`.
- **An even mix over the whole pool:** `LEVELS_CANDIDATES=6` makes six candidates per level (run it for several levels at once with `LEVELS=...` and `LEVELS_OUT=file.json`), and `LEVELS_SELECT=file1.json,file2.json` chooses one candidate per level so that the kinds of clue are spread as evenly as possible over the 18 levels and within every level (`logic/balance.ts`: the sum of the squares of how often a kind is used, in the pool and in every level; improved one level after the other). The parts of a combination count as clues of their own.
- **Gate:** `pool.test.ts` checks the plan, the stories, that both solvers verify every level, that the rater agrees with the tier, that no clue could be taken away (also not for the way in of a medium level), that no clue says nothing, that every part of a combination adds something, that a level has at most one row and one column clue, that a medium level opens the way it should, and that no two maps are equal.

## The interface

`components/RootCause.tsx` is a React island (rendered in the browser only) with two pages: the **choice of a level** (`LevelSelect.tsx`: the 18 levels as cards in three groups, and the random level) and **a level**. The choice page also explains the two looks (**Case file** and **Outage**, each with a small icon and a sentence), and every card shows its look. `?level=hard-3` in the address opens a level of the pool, the back button of the browser and the "Level selection" button at the top right of a level go between the pages, and every new page gets the focus on its heading. The moves are pure functions in `logic/play.ts`; the components only draw them.

- **Choose a person, then click a cell** (or choose with the keys 1 to 9). Choosing a person leaves the cross mode (the person is chosen to be placed or noted). The mode decides what a click does: **Place** (the same click on the same person takes them off again; somebody else on the cell is replaced), **Note** (marks the cell for the chosen person: small labels, several per cell) and **Cross** (nobody stands here). Cells blocked by an object do nothing. Under the modes there is a second row of tools: undo, redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y), clear, and the legend. Under the map there is only the list of rooms.
- **A person crosses out their row and column by themselves, and nobody else can be placed on those crosses.** (The person can still move inside their own row and column, a cross of the player does not stop anybody, and a click on such a cell is **refused visibly**: the cell shakes and flashes red for a moment, and a live region says why. The same happens for a click on a blocked cell and for a note on a cell that is crossed out or taken. With reduced motion the cell only flashes.) These crosses, and the hiding of notes, are **worked out from the placement and not stored** (`autoCrossed`, `visibleNotes`), so taking the person off brings back exactly what was there before: the crosses the player made stay, the automatic ones go, and the notes of every cell are back. A cell that is crossed out or taken shows no notes (they are kept, not deleted), and takes no new note or cross until it is free again. **Notes about a person who is placed are hidden as well** (the placement says where that person is) and come back when the person is taken off. A cross or notes under a person return when the person leaves. Once the level is solved the map is shown clean, without crosses.
- **The clues tick themselves, and turn red when they are broken.** A clue is ticked while the people placed so far satisfy it, and shown in red (a short flash, then a soft red background, a cross in the box) while a placement breaks it. Both come from the same `check` the solvers use, which answers true, false or "not decided yet" for people who are not placed; a false is final, however the rest is placed, so a red clue is never a false alarm. Moving the person takes the red away. **Satisfied clues move to the end of the list** (both groups keep the reading order), so the open ones stay together at the top. That is the only feedback while playing.
- **The level is solved** only when every person, the victim included, stands on the cell of the stored solution (the solution is the only one). Then the culprit's token pulses, the stamp ("Case closed", "Root cause found") appears and the focus moves to the result. The result takes the place of a box with a fixed height ("the case is solved when ..."), so nothing below the game moves.
- **The map** is an HTML grid (`role="grid"`, one cell is in the tab order, arrow keys move) with thick walls between rooms, window bars on the edges, and pictures of the objects (outlined: can be used, on a solid tile: blocks the cell). Most pictures are icons of the Lucide set copied into `data/object-icons.ts` (ISC license, listed in THIRD_PARTY.md); a rug, a sofa, a desk and a cable tray are our own drawings in `Glyph.tsx` (a sofa and a tray cover two cells and continue into the next one). **A room color belongs to a kind of room** (nine colors made of the site's tokens, one per room type of the skin): every server room has the same color, and two rooms next to each other of different types never do. The roster shows every person in a box of the same size (name and role, the victim with an amber edge). The rooms also carry a letter and are listed below the map, so color is never the only difference. A **legend** (a button under the map that opens a modal `<dialog>`, so nothing on the page moves) explains every picture: the objects of the level and how they are used (a chair is sat on, a rug stood on, a rack mounted in), windows, walls, people, crosses and notes. Every cell has a full label for screen readers.
- **A line under the map** says what is on the cell under the pointer (who stands on it, the room and the object) (or on the cell of the cursor, for the keyboard and for touch): the room and the object, for example "Server room 1 · Server: blocks the cell". It always takes the room of one line, so nothing moves.
- **Hints.** The **Hint** button (key H, in the second row of tools, with the number of hints used on this level) works in three stages for the same person: first a push ("think about Ada next", with how far the reasoning has to go: the clues alone, the rooms too, or what-if), then the clues are marked (the ones about the person by name, and every clue that really limits the person even without naming them, such as "The designer is not next to a desk" for the only designer; the clues that name the person only as "the other one" of a pair are in too), then the cells where the person belongs or can only be are marked, with a **"Why here?"** link that opens the reasoning in a dialog. Each stage counts as a hint; the text is in the status box below the map, so nothing moves. The hint is worked out by `deduce` in `logic/propagation.ts` from the people who stand in the right place (clues alone, then with the rules about rooms, then what-if) and `findHint` in `logic/hint.ts` takes the person that needs the least reasoning (clues, then rooms, then what-if, then the fewest cells left; suspects before the victim), so it is always the next logical step. If somebody on the map is not in the right place, the hint is about that person first ("mistake": the broken clues are marked, the third stage shows where the person belongs). **The explanation** (`logic/explain.ts`, text in `explanationLines`) is made from a trace of `deduce` (the cells of everybody after each pass of the rules): the clues that limit the person and how many cells they leave, the people on the map and the people whose own clues leave one cell (their rows and columns are taken), people who then have one cell and so on, the clues that tie people together, the rules about rooms (the victim rule and the clues about rooms and counting, quoted) and what-if, and at the end the place ("row 2, column 3"). **Only the steps that are needed are shown**: when the clues about the person alone leave one cell, the explanation is that clue and the place, nobody else is named; of the people on the map and the single cells only those are named whose rows and columns really take away cells of the person (the fewest that explain it, and when they do not explain all of it on their own, all stay so that there is no gap in the reasoning); a stage that repeats the same count is left out. Each step says how many cells are left, so nothing is made up; for a mistake the first step is the clues that the map breaks. The hint is gone as soon as the people on the map change, and the result says "Solved without a hint" or "Solved with n hints".
- **A pencil after a name** (like the check mark for a person who is placed) shows that the person has notes on the map.
- **Windows sit on the wall**: every window is the same bar (same length, thickness and distance from the ends of the wall, horizontal and vertical alike); between two rooms it is centered on the wall, half in each room, and at the edge of the map it lies flush against the outer edge (the board clips what is outside); a window belongs to the cells on both sides, so "in front of a window" and "in a room with a window" count for both rooms. **A sofa has its back to a wall** (the board edge or a room boundary): the picture is turned, along its long side and through the seam of its two cells. A chair is always drawn the same way (turning it looked too restless); a sofa and a cable tray continue across their two cells.
- **The clues** are texts made by `logic/describe.ts` from the clue and the words of the skin (`data/words.ts`: what a kind of object or a role is called). A clue names only objects that are on the map, and an "on a chair, a rug or a sofa" clue leaves out a kind that another clue of the level says nobody is on ("Nobody is on a rug"), because it would repeat it (a clue about "a chair, a rug or a sofa" on a map with only a chair says "a chair"). Anything there is only one of on the map is "the", not "a": "in the data hall", "on the rug", "The developer is not next to a desk" (a room type, an object kind or a role). "In the same row as a rack in the same room" only says that a rack lies in that row of that room: the person may stand on it or not (that is a clue of its own), also for a sofa, a chair and every other object. "Exactly 0 people" is "Nobody", and two parts of a combination share their "is" ("in a corner of their room and next to a wall"). The words follow the skin: people in Case file, **devices** in Outage ("No device is on a rack", "is mounted in a rack", "sits on a chair"). Every clue type has a test that its sentence is clean (no victim named, no placeholders, no "people" in Outage and no "device" in Case file). The clues about the map come first, then one group per suspect.
- **Random levels:** choose a difficulty (or any) and a size (or any) on the choice page and make a new level in the browser; when it is ready the level opens. The generator runs in **Web Workers** (`components/generate.worker.ts`, started by `useGenerator.ts`): up to four threads try different seeds at once and the first level that passes `problemsOf` wins, which also cuts the long waits of an unlucky map. A progress bar shows an estimate (`typicalMilliseconds` in `logic/random-level.ts`, measured on a laptop): the real time is a matter of chance, so the bar never reaches its end on its own and the text says so. Cancel ends the threads. Random levels get a title and story from templates (`data/random-stories.ts`) and an id `random-<seed>`; the seed makes the very same level again. Sizes: easy 4 to 7 (bigger easy puzzles are too rare), medium 5 to 9, hard 6 to 9. Measured on 2026-10-10 (one thread, median of 12 to 16 levels, a laptop): easy 7 by 7 about 0.8 s, medium 7 by 7 about 0.7 s, medium 9 by 9 about 10 s, hard 7 by 7 about 3 s, hard 8 by 8 about 16 s and hard 9 by 9 about 10 s, with a tail (a hard 9 by 9 has a mean of 40 s and once took three minutes). With four threads the first finished level wins: the medians were 0.1 s for easy 7 by 7, 1.3 s for medium 9 by 9, 3 s for hard 8 by 8 and 9 by 9, and the slowest tenth took up to 10 s. The estimate on the progress bar (`typicalMilliseconds`) is the single thread median divided by the number of threads to the power of 0.8. The progress is two lines: the first says what is made and how long it has taken (the number keeps its place and is not read aloud every second), the second how long it usually takes.
- **Links:** every level has a **Copy link** button. A level of the pool is `?level=easy-1`; a random level is `?level=random&seed=...&tier=...&size=...&skin=...&victim=...`, which makes the very same level again (one thread, the seed is known to work). The query is validated (`jobFromQuery`: seed of lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, tier, size 4 to 9, skin and victim role from the lists), anything else is ignored. A link only gives the same level while the generator is unchanged.
- **Why not a server:** the site is static and stores nothing; the browser's own CPU makes the levels, and leaving the page ends the work.
- **Not stored:** nothing about a game is written to the device (the address only carries `?level=`). The workers load their code from our own files (allowed by the CSP).

## Measured (2026-10-09, Case file)

Time to make a puzzle: easy 4 by 4 to 7 by 7 in well under a second up to a few seconds, medium up to 9 by 9 in a few seconds, hard up to 9 by 9 in seconds to a few minutes. That is fine for the level pool, which is made once and committed; making puzzles in the browser needs small boards, a pool, or a worker (decided with the interface). Easy puzzles on 8 by 8 and 9 by 9 are rare because easy clues alone rarely suffice, so easy levels go up to 6 by 6 or 7 by 7.

## How to test

`pnpm test` runs, next to the other tests:

- `logic/clues.test.ts` and `logic/clues-more.test.ts`: every clue type true and false on hand-made puzzles (`logic/fixtures.ts`), partial placements, the rule about the victim, the levels and the numbers of the clues.
- `logic/solver.test.ts` and `logic/propagation.test.ts`: both solvers on the hand-made puzzle, and for 100+ combinations of clues they must **agree** on the number of solutions; the rater on easy and hard sets.
- `logic/layout.test.ts`: maps for every size, tier and skin (room counts, connected and compact rooms, one corridor at most, windows on walls, objects that fit the rooms, **every room has its minimum of objects, bigger rooms and fuller kinds of rooms have more**, enough room to stand, room names).
- `logic/play.test.ts`: the moves (place, replace, remove, cross, note, clear, undo and redo, solved, the culprit, and the crosses and hidden notes that follow from a placement, including that taking the person off brings everything back) and the sentence of **every clue type** in both skins (made from generated candidates: no placeholders, never the victim's name).
- `logic/describe.test.ts`: "the" for what there is only one of, "a" for several, the new clues, "Nobody", the joined parts and the plain wording of "in the same row as".
- `logic/objects-as-units.test.ts`: next to, a direction and the same row for an object that covers two cells.
- `logic/hint.test.ts`: for every level of the pool the hints lead to the solution one logical step after the other (the person always belongs in one of the cells of the hint), an easy level needs only hints of the clues alone, a misplaced person is told first with the cell where they belong, and the texts of every stage.
- `logic/explain.test.ts`: every step of every hint of the pool starts with the clues, only narrows the cells down, ends where the hint points, reaches the cells of the hint only in its last step (no step that is not needed), names every clue that limits the person in the hint, and on an easy level uses only the plain steps; the explanation of a mistake starts with the broken clues.
- `logic/balance.test.ts`: the measure of an uneven mix and the choice of the levels.
- `logic/random-level.test.ts`: the choice of a random job, a level made for a job (verified, the wanted tier and victim, the seed makes it again), and the stories of random levels.
- `pool.test.ts`: the level pool (see above).
- `logic/generator.test.ts`: names and labels, solutions, the list of clues, a mixed set of clues, verified puzzles of every tier in both skins, neutral clues per tier, determinism, and that `problemsOf` notices a wrong level.
